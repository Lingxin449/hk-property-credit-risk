# Methodology

One-page summary of the design decisions that determine whether the reported numbers mean
anything. Read this before the README's result table.

---

## 1. Target definition

A model's ceiling is set by its label. The conventional shortcut in student credit-risk work is
to label distress by a price move (e.g. −15% in a 20-day window). That label is circular: the
market has already priced the risk by the time the label fires, so a high AUC mostly measures
how quickly prices react, not how well the model anticipates credit deterioration.

This project instead constructs an **event-driven label** from five categories of hard signal,
each anchored to the **announcement date** on HKEX (not the rumour date, not the effective
date):

| # | Signal | Anchor |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Default announcement (onshore or offshore bonds) | announcement date |
| 2 | Debt extension announcement | first announcement seeking extension |
| 3 | Distressed exchange, including restructuring support agreements | the **entry into** the agreement, not court approval or plan effectiveness |
| 4 | Winding-up petition (creditor-filed only) | announcement date |
| 5 | First rating downgrade to CCC or below (Moody's / S&P / Fitch / domestic agencies) | announcement date |

Two rules are worth flagging because they materially change the label:

- **Rating threshold is applied to the issuer rating only** (S&P ICR / Fitch IDR / Moody's CFR),
  not to individual instruments — instrument ratings are contaminated by recovery expectations.
  Moody's has no CCC notch; **Caa2 and below counts as "CCC or below", Caa1 does not.**
- **Composite events** (e.g. "default + winding-up petition" announced together) are split into
  separate rows but clustered, so they do not create artificial duplicate signals.

### Clustering and window rules

Events are first grouped per issuer into **clusters** (gap ≤ 56 calendar days → same cluster;
maximum cluster span 84 days, beyond which the cluster is forcibly split to prevent transitive
merge). With cluster anchor `a`, cluster end `e`, and next anchor `n`:

| Window | Label |
|---|---|
| `[a − 8w, a]` | y = 1 (early-warning window) |
| `(a, e + 4w]` | excluded (post-event cooldown) |
| `[e + 4w, n − 8w]` | excluded (post-event dead zone) |
| otherwise | y = 0 |

Where y = 1 and exclusion overlap, **y = 1 wins** — implemented as a boolean mask union, never as
sequential `.loc` overwrites. Excluded rows are dropped from the modelling matrix entirely rather
than relabelled as negatives.

The "exclude everything after the first event" variant was rejected: it deleted the forward
window of 38 subsequent events and nearly halved the positive class. Excluding only the dead zone
preserves the pre-event window of later events while still refusing to call a distressed issuer
"safe".

## 2. Evaluation protocol

Leakage is prevented structurally. Every boundary below is enforced in code, and the purge bands
are computed from the label width, not chosen for convenience.

```
Phase 1
  train  2019-01-01 ─── 2022-05-31 │ purge 8w │ holdout 2022-06-01 ─ 2022-12-31 │ test 2023 ─ 2024
Phase 2
  train  2019-01-01 ─── 2023-12-31 │ purge 8w │ holdout 2024-01-01 ─ 2024-12-31 │ test 2025
```

**Rules enforced:**

1. **Every fit is confined to the training segment.** Transform statistics (per-issuer z-score
   mean and standard deviation, VIF selection, winsorisation quantiles, standardisation
   parameters) are computed on training rows only. Holdout and test are transform-only.
2. **Purge bands are 8 weeks wide** — equal to the label window — at every cut point. Without
   them, a training row whose label window extends into the holdout period would leak holdout
   information into the fitted parameters. A negative result of this rule in Phase 2: the 37
   positive rows generated by January 2025 events (whose 8-week windows begin in November 2024)
   are all removed from the holdout tail, which is the main reason the Phase 2 holdout has fewer
   positives (75) than Phase 1 (137).
3. **Calibration and the decision threshold are fitted on the holdout segment only**, never on
   the test period. The threshold is frozen in the score domain and then mapped to probability,
   so the choice of calibrator cannot move the decision boundary.
4. **The test period is evaluated once.** Phase 1's test period (2023–2024) has been used and is
   now closed. In Phase 2 it is demoted to training data and 2025 becomes the new exam. This is
   disclosed because it changes what the Phase 1 AUC means: under the Phase 2 protocol, the Phase
   1 test result is an in-training-period result and can no longer be quoted as out-of-sample.
5. **Single-holdout limitation is stated, not hidden.** The holdout is 7 months (Phase 1) or 12
   months (Phase 2) with high variance. Multi-fold purged time-series CV was considered and not
   adopted; the report says plainly that conclusions rest on one holdout plus one exam.

## 3. Model and decision layer

- **Classifier:** LightGBM, fixed hyperparameters, `random_state=42`, `n_jobs=1` for Windows
  compatibility. Native handling of missing financials (delisted issuers).
- **Calibration:** fitted on the holdout segment; evaluated with Brier score and tail resolution
  in addition to ECE, because ECE alone rewards degenerate calibrators that map everything to the
  base rate.
- **Threshold:** cost-sensitive, selected in the **score domain** on the holdout segment, then
  frozen. Decision quality is therefore independent of calibrator choice.
- **Interpretability:** SHAP attributions cross-checked against a WOE/IV scorecard. Spearman rank
  correlation between the two importance rankings is 0.567 (n = 18, p = 0.014) — the two tracks
  agree on 8 core risk factors and disagree on 5.
- **Backtest:** a forward-looking-safe state machine (no position change may use information
  published after the decision date), suspension-aware (positions roll forward through trading
  halts), with hysteresis re-entry, transaction costs, and a **placebo control** that removes the
  same number of names at random each week.

## 4. What the evaluation actually showed

| Claim | Evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| The model ranks distress risk | Phase 1 test AUC 0.8621; Phase 2 event-issuer AUC 0.8615 | Supported |
| The ranking reflects a real risk ordering | Risk-tier monotonicity ρ = 0.690 / 0.840, both p < 0.0001 | Supported |
| The score converts into a better decision | Drawdown −66.5% → −44.9% | **Partly** — placebo reproduced most of it |
| The gain is from issuer selection | Placebo test | **Rejected** — gain is mainly exposure reduction |
| The result generalises beyond 2025 | Nothing yet | **Untested** |

The third and fourth rows are the point of the project. A backtest that improves drawdown while
its placebo control improves almost as much is evidence about *exposure*, not *skill* — and
reporting the distinction is more useful than the drawdown number alone.

## 5. Known weaknesses

1. **n = 44 issuers, one market, one industry.** Cross-sectional ranking is estimable;
   generalisation is not.
2. **Class imbalance and tier imbalance.** Positive rate 5.2% (Phase 1 train) to 17.3% (Phase 2
   test). The distress tier has very few issuers, so tier-level medians rest on a handful of
   companies.
3. **Only cross-sectional ordering is validated.** Temporal early-warning lead time is covered
   separately by event-level hit rate (15/17 at the frozen threshold), not by the monotonicity
   test.
4. **The decision layer is the binding constraint.** Two threshold specifications were tried; one
   beat its placebo, one did not. A third, more elaborate specification (tiered positions with a
   minimum holding period and turnover penalty) underperformed both and is reported as a negative
   result.
5. **Everything except 2025 has been seen.** Any further tuning against 2025 would destroy its
   value as an exam.

## 6. Reproduction

Code is organised so that each stage is a single entry point, and the evaluation stages read
frozen artefacts rather than refitting. A documentation consistency checker verifies that every
number quoted in the project's documents matches what the data actually produce, and that every
generated artefact is registered in the artefact index — the mechanism exists because manual
transcription of numbers into prose is the most common source of silent drift.

Raw data are vendor-licensed and not redistributed; see the README's data-availability note.
